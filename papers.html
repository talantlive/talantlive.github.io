  2. Minimal spheres and scalar curvature

    Submitted (2026).

    arXiv

    About this paper: Minimal spheres and scalar curvature
    There is a well-known principle in Riemannian geometry that a Riemannian manifold with large curvature must be "small" in some sense. A classical example of this phenomenon is the theorems Lyusternik–Schnirelmann-Toponogov, which states that every Riemannian \(2\)-sphere with Gaussian curvature \(\geq 1\) admits three distinct simple closed geodesics of length at most \(2\pi\). These closed geodesics can be thought of as "waists" of the \(2\)-sphere. We further investigate this principle in higher dimensions. We prove that if \((S^3,g)\) has positive Ricci curvature and scalar curvature \(R_g\geq\Lambda_0>0\), then there exist four distinct embedded minimal two-spheres \(\Sigma_1,\ldots,\Sigma_4\subset(S^3,g)\) such that \[ \operatorname{area}_g(\Sigma_i)\leq\frac{12\pi(i+1)}{\Lambda_0} \] for every \(i=1,\ldots,4\).

  3. Equivalence of Almgren-Pitts and phase-transition half-volume spectra

    Submitted (2026).

    arXiv

    About this paper: Equivalence of Almgren-Pitts and phase-transition half-volume spectra
    The main motivation for this paper was to contribute to the program of Liam Mazurowski and Xin Zhou toward solving the Twin Bubble Conjecture, which asks whether every closed Riemannian manifold of dimension between \(3\) and \(7\) admits at least two distinct closed hypersurfaces with any prescribed positive constant mean curvature. The main idea of their approach is to study the half-volume spectrum, a min-max construction related to constant mean curvature hypersurfaces enclosing half the volume of the manifold. There are two ways to define this spectrum: using Almgren–Pitts theory and using phase transitions (the Allen–Cahn equation). In this paper, we prove that these two spectra are equal, confirming a conjecture of Liam Mazurowski and Xin Zhou; see here.

  5. Generic density of stationary geodesic nets that are not closed geodesics

    Submitted (2025).

    arXiv

    About this paper: Generic density of stationary geodesic nets that are not closed geodesics
    Stationary geodesic nets are graphs whose edges are geodesic segments, with the unit velocity vectors summing to zero at each vertex. Closed geodesics are the simplest examples of such nets. Interestingly, not many examples beyond closed geodesics were previously known. Alexander Nabutovsky and Fabian Parsch asked whether every Riemannian manifold admits a stationary geodesic net that is not a closed geodesic; see here. We prove that, for a Baire-generic Riemannian metric on a closed smooth manifold of dimension at least \(3\), the union of stationary geodesic nets that are not closed geodesics is dense in the manifold.

  6. Besicovitch-type inequality for closed geodesics on 2-dimensional spheres

    To appear in Journal of Topology and Analysis (2024).

    arXiv

    About this paper: Besicovitch-type inequality for closed geodesics on 2-dimensional spheres
    We prove the existence of a constant \(C > 0\) such that for any Riemannian metric \(g\) on a \(2\)-dimensional sphere \(S^2\), there exist two distinct closed geodesics with lengths \(L_{1}\) and \(L_{2}\) satisfying \(L_{1} L_{2} \leq C \cdot \operatorname{Area}(S^2, g)\). This answers a question posed by Yevgeny Liokumovich; see here. This result can be seen as a spherical analog of the classical Besicovitch inequality, which provides a bound on the product of distances between opposite sides of a Riemannian square in terms of its area.

  7. The Gromov-Hausdorff Distance between Vertex Sets of Regular Polygons Inscribed in a Single Circle

    Moscow University Mathematics Bulletin 78 (2023), no. 3, 130–135.

    Journal·arXiv

    About this paper: The Gromov-Hausdorff Distance between Vertex Sets of Regular Polygons Inscribed in a Single Circle
    We calculate the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between vertex sets of regular polygons endowed with the round metric. We give a full answer for the case of \(n\)- and \(m\)-gons with \(m\) divisible by \(n\). Also, we calculate all distances to \(2\)-gons and \(3\)-gons. This is a part of my undergraduate thesis, completed under the supervision of Alexey A. Tuzhilin.
